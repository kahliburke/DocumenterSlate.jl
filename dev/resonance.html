Resonance ​

Push the damped oscillator with a periodic force of frequency , measured in units of its natural frequency, and once the start-up transient has died away it oscillates at the driving frequency. How far it swings depends on how close is to 1, and on the damping ratio : the same that sets how quickly its free oscillation decays.

julia
A(Ω, ζ) = 1 / sqrt((1 - Ω^2)^2 + (2ζ * Ω)^2)
Ωs = collect(range(0, 3; length = 300))
Ωpeak = zeta < 1 / sqrt(2) ? sqrt(1 - 2zeta^2) : 0.0
nothing
julia
echart(:line, Ωs, @replay(zeta, [A(Ω, zeta) for Ω in Ωs]);
       name = "A(Ω)", showSymbol = false,
       xAxis = (type = :value, name = "Ω"), yAxis = (type = :log, name = "amplitude"))

Quality factor ​

Light damping gives a tall, narrow peak. Its sharpness is the quality factor  , which for light damping is also roughly the number of cycles the free oscillation takes to lose most of its energy.

julia
slate_table([(ζ = z, Q = round(1 / (2z); digits = 2), peak = round(A(sqrt(max(1 - 2z^2, 0.0)), z); digits = 2))
             for z in (0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5)])
