Resonance

Push the damped oscillator with a periodic force of frequency $\Omega$, measured in units of its natural frequency, and once the start-up transient has died away it oscillates at the driving frequency. How far it swings depends on how close $\Omega$ is to 1, and on the damping ratio $\zeta$: the same $\zeta$ that sets how quickly its free oscillation decays.

\[A(\Omega) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{(1-\Omega^2)^2 + (2\zeta\Omega)^2}}\]

A(Ω, ζ) = 1 / sqrt((1 - Ω^2)^2 + (2ζ * Ω)^2)Ωs = collect(range(0, 3; length = 300))Ωpeak = zeta < 1 / sqrt(2) ? sqrt(1 - 2zeta^2) : 0.0nothing

Quality factor

Light damping gives a tall, narrow peak. Its sharpness is the quality factor $Q = 1/(2\zeta)$, which for light damping is also roughly the number of cycles the free oscillation takes to lose most of its energy.

slate_table([(ζ = z, Q = round(1 / (2z); digits = 2), peak = round(A(sqrt(max(1 - 2z^2, 0.0)), z); digits = 2))             for z in (0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5)])
